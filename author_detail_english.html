Walt Whitman

Poet • Essayist • Journalist
Free Verse Lyrical Poetry Individualism

Introduction

Walt Whitman is considered one of the most influential poets in American literature. He is also called the 'People's poet' as well as the most individualistic literary figure that America has ever produced. Art Historian Mary Berenson wrote, "You cannot really understand America without Walt Whitman, without Leaves of Grass."

Personal Information

  • Born: May 31, 1819
  • Died: March 26, 1892
  • Birth place: America
  • Resting place: Harleigh Cemetery Camden, New Jersey, U.S.
  • Nationality: American
  • Education: Left formal schooling at age 11

Early Life and Career

Whitman began working as a carpenter before his twelfth birthday - at the age of 11, he left formal schooling to go to work. He worked in various professions including journalist, teacher, printer, editor, librarian, and government clerk.

During the American Civil War in late December 1862, he went to Washington, D.C. and worked in hospitals caring for the wounded as a volunteer nurse, demonstrating his compassion and dedication to serving others.

Works

  • Leaves of Grass (1855) - His masterpiece collection
  • Drum Taps (1866)
  • "Song of Myself" - 52 sections with approximately 1300 lines

Literary Significance

Leaves of Grass is known for its free rhythms and lack of rhyme. However, the free form of the poems and joyful dedications to the 'importance of the individual' were not well received at first. His collection of poems cost Whitman his job, as it was taken to be obscene.

Whitman first published it at his own expense in 1855 because he found no publisher willing to print it uncensored. In 1881, after many editions, Leaves of Grass finally found a publisher willing to print uncensored.

Translations of this collection were enthusiastically received in Europe, but Whitman remained relatively unappreciated in America during his lifetime. It was only after his death that he gained appreciation in America for his original and innovative expression.

His poem "Song of Myself" is a free verse poem based on universal brotherhood, freedom of speech, and patriotism. At the age of 73, his funeral was a public event.

W. H. Auden

Poet • Dramatist • Playwright • Critic • Translator • Editor
20th Century Poetry British Phase American Phase

Introduction

W. H. Auden won the Pulitzer Prize in 1948 for his collection "The Age of Anxiety". He is called the 20th Century's vigorous poet and was known as a multifaceted literary figure working as a dramatist, playwright, critic, translator, and editor.

Poetry to him was a "Serious Game" - he had a passion for word coining and using archaic words which makes him, at times, difficult to understand. He left England as early as 1939 to settle permanently in the United States.

Personal Information

  • Born: February 21, 1907
  • Died: September 29, 1973
  • Birth place: York, England
  • Death place: Vienna, Austria
  • Shifted place: America (1939)
  • Nationality: British-American

Literary Phases

Auden's works are divided into the British and American phases, reflecting his geographical and cultural transitions.

Works

Notable Works of the English Phase

  • The Orators
  • The Dance of The Death
  • Look Stranger
  • Spain
  • Another Time

Notable Works of the American Phase

  • New Year Letter
  • For the Time Being
  • The Age of Anxiety (Pulitzer Prize winner, 1948)
  • The Shield of Achilles

Recognition

Auden received numerous accolades throughout his career, with the Pulitzer Prize being one of his most significant achievements. His innovative approach to poetry and his ability to capture the anxieties of the modern age made him one of the most influential poets of the 20th century.

John Keats

Poet • Romantic Poet
Romanticism Odes Greek Mythology

Introduction

John Keats was one of the greatest romantic poets of English literature. His ambition to become a poet was fired by his first acquaintance with Spencer's Faerie Queene. He was a voracious reader of Greek Mythology and showed deep interest in cricket and boxing.

Keats started a translation of Virgil's Aeneid and achieved remarkable literary greatness despite his short life span. His poems and letters have depth beneath their beauty and reflect his awareness of the pains and sufferings of life.

Personal Information

  • Born: 1795
  • Died: 1821 (age 26)
  • Birth place: Moon fields, London
  • Education: Clark's school at Enfield
  • Cause of death: Tuberculosis
  • Nationality: English

Works

  • "Lines in Imitation of Spencer" (1814) - His first published poem
  • "Poems" (1817) - His first volume of poetry
  • "Endymion" (1818) - Well appreciated by William Wordsworth
  • "On Indolence"
  • "On a Grecian Urn"
  • "To Psyche"
  • "To a Nightingale"
  • "On Melancholy"
  • "Ode to Autumn"
  • "Lamia"
  • "Hyperion"

Famous Quotes

Keats is remembered for his profound philosophical statements:
"A thing of beauty is a joy forever."
"Beauty is truth, truth is beauty."

Legacy

Despite dying of tuberculosis at the young age of 26, Keats was able to achieve greatness within his short span of life. His odes made him a great romantic poet, and his works continue to inspire readers with their beauty and depth.

Walter de la Mare

Poet • Writer
Children's Poetry Supernatural Melancholy

Introduction

Walter de la Mare was born in the village of Charlton in Kent, England. He lost his father at the age of four and had to move with his family to London. He is known for his sensitive concerns with the world of children and the world of the supernatural.

His Songs for Children and Peacock Pie depict a world of dreams, but even in these songs there is a note of 'melancholy'. The poet makes us aware of the inhuman condition of modern life.

Personal Information

  • Born: 1873
  • Died: 1956
  • Birth place: Charlton, Kent, England
  • Family tragedy: Lost father at age 4
  • Nationality: English

Works

  • Songs for Children
  • Peacock Pie
  • "An Epitaph" (mentioned poem)

Recognition

He showed his genius by evincing fresh interest in old themes. Queen Elizabeth II honoured him with the Order of Merit in 1953, recognizing his significant contributions to English literature.

Literary Style

De la Mare's work is characterized by his exploration of both the innocent world of children and the mysterious realm of the supernatural, often infused with a subtle melancholy that reflects the complexities of modern existence.

Rupert Brooke

Poet • War Poet
First World War War Sonnets Patriotism

Introduction

Rupert Brooke was born in a well-to-do academic family. His father was a housemaster at Rugby School, where Rupert was educated before going to King's College Cambridge. W. B. Yeats, the famous poet, described him as "the handsomest young man in England."

He assumed a symbolic role that eventually turned into the myth of a young and beautiful fallen warrior. Brooke is remembered as a 'war poet' who inspired patriotism in the early phase of the First World War.

Personal Information

  • Born: 1887
  • Died: 1915
  • Education: Rugby School, King's College Cambridge
  • Father's profession: Housemaster at Rugby School
  • Cause of death: Septicaemia
  • Death location: Off the island of Lemnos in the Aegean
  • Death date: April 23, 1915
  • Nationality: English

Works

  • Five War Sonnets
  • "The Soldier" - His most famous and anthologized poem

Death and Legacy

He died of septicaemia on April 23, 1915, off the island of Lemnos in the Aegean on his way to a battle at Gavipoli during the First World War. His reputation chiefly rests on five war sonnets of which "The Soldier" is undoubtedly the best and the most anthologised.

Recognition

Brooke became an iconic figure representing the tragedy of young lives lost in World War I, embodying the idealism and patriotic fervor of the early war period before the harsh realities of trench warfare became widely known.

T. Stearns Eliot

Poet • Critic • Dramatist
20th Century Modernism Nobel Prize Winner

Introduction

Thomas Stearns Eliot was born in St. Louis, Missouri, U.S.A., but eventually settled in England. A classicist in literature, an Anglo-Catholic in religion, and a royalist in politics, he rose to become one of the greatest poets of the twentieth century.

He was also a distinguished critic and dramatist. He was awarded the Nobel Prize for literature in 1948. As a poet and spokesman for his age, he truly represents the intellectual, social and philosophical moods of the twentieth century.

Personal Information

  • Born: 1888
  • Died: 1965
  • Birthplace: St. Louis, Missouri, U.S.A.
  • Settled in: England
  • Nationality: American-British
  • Religion: Anglo-Catholic
  • Political views: Royalist

Works

  • The Waste Land
  • Ash Wednesday
  • Four Quartets

Recognition

Eliot was awarded the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1948, recognizing his outstanding contribution to poetry and literary criticism. His works continue to influence modern literature and represent the complex intellectual landscape of the 20th century.

Literary Significance

His poetry captures the fragmentation and disillusionment of the modern world, particularly in works like "The Waste Land", which is considered one of the most important poems of the 20th century.

Keki N. Daruwalla

Poet • Short Story Writer • Police Officer
Landscape Poetry Subjective Response Rural Landscape

Introduction

Keki Naseerwanji Daruwalla left undivided India in 1945 and settled at Junagarh in Gujarat. He was recipient of Sahitya Akademi Award (1984) and Commonwealth Poetry Award, is a landscape poet of eminence and a well-known writer of short stories.

The themes of his poetry are love, death, domination, cynicism, plight of human society and violence. He writes with intensity and vigour involved in poetic creation.

Personal Information

  • Born: 1937
  • Died: 2024
  • Nationality: Indian
  • Settled in: Junagarh, Gujarat
  • Career: Indian Police Service (joined 1958)

Literary Style

His poetry is, in his own words, 'a totally impressionistic recording of subjective responses'. He claims his poems to be 'rooted in the rural landscape' and hence 'earthy'.

He shuns sophistication, as he thinks that it, 'while adding gloss, takes away the power of verse'. Since he had been in police service, violence is unavoidable in his poetry.

  • Commonwealth Poetry Award

Works

His important volumes of verse include:

  • Under Orion (1970)

Mahatma Gandhi

Political Leader • Philosopher • Writer
Non-violence Truth Indian Independence

Introduction

He was popularly known as Bapu or the Father of the Nation, was more a spiritual leader than a politician. He successfully used truth and non-violence as the chief weapons against the British Rule in India and helped India gain independence.

From 1915 till 1948, he completely dominated Indian politics. He died at the hands of a fanatic. His autobiography, 'My Experiments with Truth', and the numerous articles that he wrote for 'Young India' and the speeches that he delivered on different occasions, reveal him not only as an original thinker but also as a great master of chaste, idiomatic English.

Personal Information

  • Born: 1869
  • Died: 1948
  • Nationality: Indian
  • Known as: Bapu, Father of the Nation

Works

  • My Experiments with Truth (Autobiography)
  • Articles for 'Young India'
  • Numerous speeches and writings
  • Indian Civilization and Culture

Philosophy and Methods

Gandhi's philosophy was centered around Satyagraha (truth-force) and Ahimsa (non-violence). He believed these were the most powerful weapons for achieving social and political change without causing harm.

Legacy

Gandhi's impact extended far beyond India's independence movement. His methods of peaceful resistance influenced civil rights movements worldwide and continue to inspire leaders fighting for justice and equality across the globe.

Martin Luther King Jr.

Baptist Minister • Civil Rights Activist • Social Reformer
Nonviolent Resistance Civil Rights Movement Nobel Peace Prize

Introduction

Martin Luther King Jr. was a Baptist minister by training, who became a civil rights activist early in his career. He led the Montgomery Bus Boycott (5 Dec. 1955- 20 Dec 1956) and helped to found the Southern Christian Leadership Conference.

Influenced by Gandhi, his philosophy of non-violent resistance brought him worldwide attention. His leadership in the American civil rights movement made him one of the most important figures in the fight for racial equality.

Personal Information

  • Born: 15 January, 1929
  • Died: 4 April, 1968
  • Nationality: American
  • Profession: Baptist Minister, Civil Rights Leader
  • Death location: Memphis, Tennessee

Major Achievements

  • Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955-1956) - Leadership
  • Southern Christian Leadership Conference - Co-founder
  • Nobel Peace Prize (1964) - Youngest recipient at the time
  • "I Have a Dream" speech - Iconic civil rights moment

Recognition

In 1964, King became the youngest person to receive the Nobel Prize for his efforts to end segregation and racial discrimination through civil disobedience movement and other non-violent ways.

Death

King was assassinated on April 4, 1968 by James Earl Ray in Memphis, Tennessee at Lorraine Motel's balcony. His death was a tragic loss for the civil rights movement and American society.

Legacy

Martin Luther King Jr.'s philosophy of nonviolent resistance, inspired by Mahatma Gandhi, transformed the American civil rights movement and continues to inspire social justice movements worldwide. His dream of racial equality and justice remains a guiding principle for activists around the globe.
