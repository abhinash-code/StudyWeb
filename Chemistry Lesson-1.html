Lesson 1 - Solid State

1. Classification of Solids

Crystalline vs amorphous solids, differences in structure, properties and examples of each type with real-world applications.


2. Crystalline Solid

A solid in which particles are arranged in a regular, repeating, three-dimensional pattern known as long-range order. These solids have sharp melting points, well-defined geometric shapes, and diffract X-rays due to ordered arrangement. They are anisotropic, meaning their properties vary with direction.


3. Amorphous Solid

Solids that lack long-range order; particles are randomly arranged with only short-range order. They are isotropic (properties same in all directions), lack sharp melting points, and soften gradually over a range of temperatures—behaving like “supercooled liquids.”


4. Crystal lattice

The crystal lattice is an infinite array of points depicting the geometric positions in a crystalline solid. The crystal structure is formed when a specific motif or basis is placed at each lattice point and repeated throughout space, defining the symmetry and properties of the material.


5. Unit Cell

The smallest repeating unit of a crystal lattice that, when translated through space, reproduces the entire lattice. It reflects the full symmetry of the crystal and is defined by lattice parameters (edge lengths and angles).


6. Primitive and Non Primitive Cell

A primitive unit cell contains only one lattice point (one basis) and is the smallest possible volume. A non-primitive (conventional) unit cell may contain more than one lattice point, chosen for convenience to reflect symmetry.


7. Bravais Lattice

A set of infinite discrete points in space having identical surroundings, generated by integer combinations of fundamental translation vectors. It’s the mathematical backbone of crystal structures.


8. Packing Efficiency

The fraction of volume in a unit cell occupied by constituent particles, compared to the total cell volume. In solid state, SC ≈ 52.4%, BCC ≈ 68%, and FCC/CCP ≈ 74%.


9. Crystallographic Deffect

Any disruption in the regular pattern of a crystal lattice, including point defects (vacancies, interstitials, substitutional impurities), line defects (dislocations), planar defects (grain boundaries), and bulk defects. These affect physical properties like conductivity and strength.


10. Vacancy

A type of point defect where a lattice site that should be occupied is empty. It causes local distortion and affects properties like density and diffusion.


11.interstitial Defect

A defect where a particle occupies a normally unoccupied position between lattice sites. This can strain the crystal and alter its properties.


12. Color Center(F-Center)

A point defect typically in ionic crystals where an anion vacancy traps an unpaired electron. This defect absorbs visible light, giving the crystal color.


13. X-ray Diffraction

X-ray diffraction is a non-destructive technique to determine crystal structure by measuring diffraction patterns from lattice planes. Bragg’s law (nλ = 2d sin θ) relates wavelength and interplanar spacing.


14. Crystallinity

The measure of structural order in a solid. A higher degree of crystallinity means more regular periodic arrangement of particles, which significantly influences properties—hardness, density, transparency.





हिंदी में


1. ठोस

वह पदार्थ जिसकी निश्चित आकृति और आयतन होता है, उसे ठोस कहते हैं। इसमें कण बहुत सघन रूप से जुड़े होते हैं और केवल कंपन कर सकते हैं।


2. ठोस के प्रकार

क्रिस्टलीय ठोस: जिनमें कण एक निश्चित क्रम में व्यवस्थित होते हैं।
अमॉर्फ ठोस: जिनमें कण अनियमित रूप से व्यवस्थित होते हैं।


3. क्रिस्टल लैटिस

त्रि-आयामी ज्यामितीय व्यवस्था जिसमें सभी बिंदु एक जैसे परिवेश में होते हैं, उसे क्रिस्टल लैटिस कहते हैं।


4. यूनिट सेल

क्रिस्टल लैटिस का सबसे छोटा भाग, जिसे दोहराने पर पूरा क्रिस्टल बनता है, यूनिट सेल कहलाता है।


5. यूनिट सेल के प्रकार

प्रिमिटिव: केवल कोनों पर कण।
बॉडी सेंटर्ड (BCC): कोनों के साथ केंद्र में कण।
फेस सेंटर्ड (FCC): कोनों और प्रत्येक फेस के केंद्र में कण।


6. समन्वय संख्या

किसी कण के सबसे नज़दीकी कणों की संख्या को समन्वय संख्या कहते हैं।


7. रिक्त स्थान / शून्य

किसी ठोस में पैकिंग के कारण बची हुई खाली जगह को रिक्त स्थान या void कहते हैं।


8. पैकिंग दक्षता

क्रिस्टल में उपलब्ध कुल स्थान की तुलना में कणों द्वारा घेर लिया गया स्थान पैकिंग दक्षता कहलाता है।


9. बिंदु दोष

क्रिस्टल के कुछ स्थानों पर कणों की कमी या अतिरिक्त उपस्थिति को बिंदु दोष कहते हैं।
प्रकार: स्टॉइकियोमेट्रिक दोष, गैर-स्टॉइकियोमेट्रिक दोष, अशुद्धि दोष।


10. विद्युत गुण

ठोस विद्युत चालक (metals), अर्धचालक (semiconductors) और कुचालक (insulators) हो सकते हैं।


11. चुम्बकीय गुण

ठोस को चुम्बकीय गुणों के आधार पर पाँच भागों में बाँटा जाता है: डायमैग्नेटिक, पैरामैग्नेटिक, फेरोमैग्नेटिक, एंटी-फेरोमैग्नेटिक और फेर्रीमैग्नेटिक।


12. एक्स-रे विवर्तन विधि

क्रिस्टल की संरचना जानने के लिए एक्स-रे का प्रयोग किया जाता है। इसे ब्रैग का नियम द्वारा समझा जाता है: nλ = 2d sinθ


Essential Formulas - Solid State

Density of Crystal

ρ = (Z × M) / (NA × a3)

Where Z = number of atoms per unit cell, M = molar mass (g/mol), NA = Avogadro's number (6.022×1023), a = lattice parameter (cm)

Simple Cubic (SC)

a = 2r

Relation between atomic radius (r) and lattice parameter (a) in simple cubic structure with coordination number 6.

Body-Centered Cubic (BCC)

a = 4r / √3

Relation in BCC structure (coordination number 8) where body diagonal contains 4 atomic radii.

Face-Centered Cubic (FCC)

a = 2√2 r

Relation in FCC structure (coordination number 12) where face diagonal contains 4 atomic radii.

Packing Efficiencies

SC ≈ 52.4%
BCC ≈ 68%
FCC ≈ 74%

Percentage of unit cell volume occupied by atoms. FCC has highest efficiency among cubic structures.

Bragg's Law

nλ = 2d sinθ

For cubic crystals: dhkl = a / √(h² + k² + l²) where (hkl) are Miller indices of crystal planes.

Density of Unit Cell

ρ = (Z × M) / (NA × a3)

Z = number of atoms per unit cell, M = molar mass, a = edge length, NA = Avogadro number

Simple Cubic (SC)

a = 2r

Edge length is equal to 2 times the atomic radius.

Body Centered Cubic (BCC)

a = 4r / √3

Derived from the body diagonal relation. A unit cell contains 2 atoms (Z = 2).

Face Centered Cubic (FCC)

a = 4r / √2

In FCC/CCP system, there are 4 atoms per unit cell (Z = 4).

Packing Efficiency (SC)

(π/6) × 100 = 52.4%

Packing efficiency of simple cubic structure is 52.4%.

Packing Efficiency (BCC)

68%

Packing efficiency of body centered cubic cell.

Packing Efficiency (FCC/CCP)

74%

FCC/Cubic close packing has the highest packing efficiency.

Number of Atoms in Unit Cell

SC = 1    BCC = 2    FCC = 4

Number of atoms present in different cubic structures.

Voids in Close Packing

Tetrahedral voids = 2N
Octahedral voids = N

For N close packed spheres, the number of tetrahedral and octahedral voids formed.

Radius Ratio Rule

r+ / r-

The coordination number of an ionic crystal depends on the radius ratio of cation to anion.

Schottky Defect Effect

Density ↓

Due to missing ions, the density of the crystal decreases.

Frenkel Defect Effect

Density constant

Cations shift to interstitial positions. Density remains unchanged.
